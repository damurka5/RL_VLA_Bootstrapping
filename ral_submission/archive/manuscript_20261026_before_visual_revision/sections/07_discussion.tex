\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
The central question is how to adapt VLA-based control when an existing action interface does not provide useful behavior on a new robot. Dense reward supports skill discovery, binary refinement targets completion, self-imitation consolidates experience, and staged RL learns the complete instruction. These roles require separate measurements of acquisition, retention, and composition.

Our experiments examine a common mechanism: ordered task achievements distinguish trajectories that share the same terminal failure. CDPR exposes these distinctions through milestone advantages; LIBERO also resolves progress within a phase. The observed dense advantage supports intermediate feedback beyond initial acquisition. Its granularity remains an empirical choice: our LIBERO ablations do not isolate progress density, and a matched dense-versus-milestone CDPR comparison remains outstanding.

Binary skill refinement and milestone composition serve different objectives and reset distributions. Our schedule uses binary rewards to target skill completion, then reintroduces structured feedback for the longer composed task. Our LIBERO results support this rationale; they do not establish that switching to binary rewards, or the full schedule, is superior to continued dense training.

The CDPR is one embodiment-adaptation case, using a new residual over a retained SmolVLA expert. Resetting an entire expert or applying the full schedule to additional embodiments remains untested; LIBERO starts with a trained policy. Skill consolidation also gives no guarantee that further SFT on full-task successes helps: unsuccessful campaign attempts show the importance of data mixture, coverage, and action consistency.

The current CDPR endpoint uses development scenes and one training lineage. Matched schedule, self-imitation, and composition ablations, independent seeds, and a locked final test remain necessary for stronger attribution. Our LIBERO evaluation requires completed trial denominators and quantitative throughput records. Physical success remains a drafting assumption until trial evidence is inserted. Manual rewards, historical scripted initialization, calibration, and inherited training costs remain part of the method's scope.
