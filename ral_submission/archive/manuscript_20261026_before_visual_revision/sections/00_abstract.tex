\begin{abstract}
Adapting a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model to a new embodiment can require learning an action module for the target controller. Sparse task rewards provide little feedback when this module initially produces few successful trajectories. We propose a staged reinforcement learning approach that first uses dense rewards to acquire individual manipulation skills, then refines them with binary success rewards and consolidates successful trajectories through self-imitation. Milestone-based reinforcement learning subsequently composes the skills into a policy conditioned on the complete task instruction. Our approach exposes ordered task progress through phase-aware dense rewards or discrete milestones, with stage-specific credit during composition. We evaluate reward construction with OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO and the adaptation schedule with a residual action module over pretrained SmolVLA on a cable-driven parallel manipulator. Phase-aware dense feedback improves the trained LIBERO policy under the tested settings; the adapted cable-robot controller achieves 61.43\% strict full-task success in simulation development evaluation. \assumed{Physical experiments demonstrate complete object manipulation on the target robot.} These experiments examine structured feedback for acquiring and composing manipulation skills across different action interfaces.
\end{abstract}
