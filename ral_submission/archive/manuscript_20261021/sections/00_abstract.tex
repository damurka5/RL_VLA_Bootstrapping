\begin{abstract}
Binary task rewards provide little learning signal when robot rollouts fail at different stages of manipulation. We study stage-aware feedback in two complementary vision-language-action (VLA) reinforcement-learning settings. On LIBERO-Spatial, a phase-aware reward augments terminal verification with bounded credit for new within-phase progress and completed phases. On a cable-driven parallel robot (CDPR), a SmolVLA-conditioned residual controller is bootstrapped from skill experience and optimized with three-stage group-relative credit, protection of achieved milestones from negative completion bonuses, and conditional latent-action likelihoods. In the supplied LIBERO study, dense feedback reaches 65.3\% validation success from a 61.0\% initialization, compared with 61.7\% for terminal-only training on two NVIDIA L40 GPUs. In matched CDPR development evaluation, the 40M continuation achieves 1,258/2,048 strict successes (61.43\%), versus 51.56\% at 20M; the paired improvement is 9.86 percentage points with a 95\% interval of [7.37, 12.30]. These studies examine different implementations and do not constitute a cross-backbone controlled comparison. \assumed{Physical deployment demonstrates complete pick-and-place on the cable robot; trial counts, measured success, and deployment conditions are to be inserted after verification.}
\end{abstract}
