\begin{abstract}
Adapting a vision-language-action (VLA) model to a new robot can require replacing or reinitializing its action head. The model must then learn how to act on the new embodiment, often before successful task executions are available. We propose a simple training sequence for this setting. First, reinforcement learning with dense rewards teaches the individual skills of a manipulation task by providing feedback on partial progress. Next, binary rewards focus each skill on successful completion, while self-imitation consolidates successful experience. Finally, staged reinforcement learning combines these skills into one policy that executes the complete instruction. We study this approach with SmolVLA on a cable-driven manipulator, chosen as an example of an unusual robot embodiment. The adapted controller achieves 61.43\% strict full-task success in simulation development evaluation. A complementary OpenVLA-OFT experiment on LIBERO examines stage-aware feedback for an already trained manipulator. \assumed{Physical experiments demonstrate complete object manipulation on the target robot.} Together, the studies investigate how to build useful robot behavior from pretrained VLA representations through a sequence of learnable steps.
\end{abstract}
