\begin{abstract}
We adapt a pretrained vision-language-action model to a simulated four-cable robot without collecting human demonstrations. We train a new residual controller and adapt the action expert with low-rank updates. Dense rewards teach individual skills. Binary rewards refine them. Self-imitation on saved successes helps recover skills lost during training. We then train the complete task with milestones for approach, pickup, and placement. Training uses policy-generated, scripted, and assisted trajectories. The robot completes 1,416 of 2,048 unassisted development attempts (69.14\%). In a checkpoint audit, self-imitation raises reaching success from 9.0\% to 34.1\%. Transferring the updated residual alone reproduces that gain.
\end{abstract}
