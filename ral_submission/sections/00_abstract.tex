\begin{abstract}
Can a pretrained vision-language-action model learn to pick and place on a new robot without human demonstrations on that robot? We retain the pretrained action expert and train a residual controller in simulation. We divide the instruction into skills for which progress is easy to measure. Dense rewards teach the first movements, binary rewards refine each skill, and self-imitation on saved successes helps recover skills lost during training. We then relabel trajectories with one full-task instruction and reuse the skill success conditions as milestones for reinforcement learning. On a four-cable robot, training also used scripted and assisted trajectories. The policy completed 1,416 of 2,048 unassisted development attempts (69.14\%). On a simulated WidowX-200, placement from an already held object succeeded in 35.64\% of attempts, but completion from an empty gripper reached only 7.13\%. The difference illustrates why composition must be tested from the start of the task.
\end{abstract}
