\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Vision-language-action (VLA) models provide useful visual and language representations for robot control, but adaptation to a new embodiment commonly relies on demonstrations collected with the target robot~\cite{smolvla,openvlaoft}. For custom robots, collecting these data requires a suitable control interface and repeated demonstrations of the desired behavior. We study adaptation that begins with reinforcement learning (RL), without collecting human demonstrations on the target embodiment. The difficulty is that a policy transferred to a new action interface may rarely complete the task, leaving binary success with little guidance for learning.

Consider \emph{put the apple on the plate}. A failed rollout may contain a correct approach and grasp followed by a poor release. Treating the entire attempt as a failure discards this distinction. Training the parts separately helps exploration, but a policy can forget earlier skills while learning later ones. It must then connect the retained skills into a complete behavior.

We address these problems through the pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}. Dense rewards are used only at the beginning to acquire a base policy for simple skills. Binary refinement then targets skill completion, while balanced self-imitation retains successful behavior across instructions. Finally, staged RL trains this policy to execute the complete multi-stage task from an empty-gripper start, using binary milestones throughout full-task training.

We evaluate the adaptation pipeline in cable-driven parallel robot (CDPR) simulation using SmolVLA~\cite{smolvla}, LoRA, and a newly initialized residual controller. This experiment measures skill retention and complete-task performance on the target action interface. A complementary experiment uses OpenVLA-OFT~\cite{openvlaoft} on LIBERO-Spatial to compare terminal and intermediate feedback under a common training setup. It examines the feedback used to guide multi-stage behavior, while the CDPR experiment evaluates the complete acquisition, consolidation, and composition procedure.

We contribute a training schedule that begins target-robot adaptation with dense-reward skill acquisition, followed by binary refinement with instruction-balanced self-imitation and staged full-task learning under one instruction. We specify milestone credit that retains local credit for achieved approach and pickup stages when later placement fails. Our experiments compare intermediate and terminal feedback, measure skill recovery after sequential training, and report complete-task performance after action-interface adaptation.
