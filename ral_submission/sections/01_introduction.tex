\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Vision-language-action (VLA) models turn images and instructions into robot actions. They often learn from large demonstration datasets. For example, OpenVLA was pretrained on 970,000 robot demonstrations~\cite{openvla}. We study adaptation to a cable-driven parallel robot (CDPR) without collecting human demonstrations on it. Four cables support the gripper. We adapt the pretrained action expert with low-rank updates and train a small residual network to correct its commands.

Early attempts rarely complete the task. A reward for full success gives little feedback at this stage. We first teach approach, pickup, and placement as separate skills. Dense rewards measure progress on each skill. Binary rewards then refine successful behavior~\cite{simplevlarl}. We save successes and use them to recover skills that decline during later training.

We reuse the saved actions under one instruction. The same skill success conditions become milestones for full-task reinforcement learning (RL). Training in simulation also uses scripted and assisted trajectories. We test full-task execution from an empty gripper. We also compare saved checkpoints to see which parts of the policy change during skill recovery.
