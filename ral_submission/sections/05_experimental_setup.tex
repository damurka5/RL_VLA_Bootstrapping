\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate skill retention and complete-task adaptation in CDPR simulation, and study intermediate feedback separately on LIBERO-Spatial.

The CDPR simulation uses MuJoCo Warp/MJ-Lab, four object categories (apple, orange, potato, and tomato), and plate or bowl destinations. The action interface controls Cartesian translation, yaw, and the gripper; a fixed inverse-kinematics controller converts Cartesian targets to cable lengths.

No human demonstrations are collected on the CDPR. Skill consolidation uses successful policy rollouts. Before full-task RL, the policy is additionally fitted to automatically generated complete-task trajectories from a scripted oracle and assisted rollouts of learned skills. Full-task RL and evaluation use unassisted empty-gripper starts.

\subsection{SmolVLA: Complete-Task Adaptation}\label{sec:cdpr_results}
Strict success requires grasp, held lift, intentional release, and correct placement, without carry slip or wrong-placement events.

Development evaluation uses 512 distinct scenes, each repeated four times, for 2,048 full-task attempts: 1,028 plate and 1,020 bowl attempts. The fixed 128-scene validation panel used during training is excluded. Each attempt starts with an empty gripper and uses one full-task instruction, without teacher assistance. Residual actions are deterministic while the VLA prior retains unit-scale sampling noise. All attempts, including numerical failures, remain in the denominator. These development scenes support model selection; the final-test split remains unused.
