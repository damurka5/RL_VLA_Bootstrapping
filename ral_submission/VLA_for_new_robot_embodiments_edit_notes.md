## Substantive corrections to existing text

| Draft statement or annotation | Correction and reason |
|---|---|
| A general 60–70% starting success rate and improvement to 100%. | The 60–70% range is not a general property of VLAs. It was replaced with OpenVLA's documented pretraining size. The 100% claim is limited to selected reported tasks and linked to PLD. |
| Similar 6-DoF robots require only a few parameters to transfer skills. | Parameter-efficient adaptation and the bimanual example are retained, without asserting a universal robot geometry or an amount of fine-tuning sufficient for transfer. |
| A 5-DoF cable robot. | The text names three translation axes, yaw, and gripper opening. This avoids treating the gripper command as a fifth spatial degree of freedom. |
| The action expert/head must be reinitialized and trained from scratch; changing only the last layer or excluding output channels does not work. | The evaluated SmolVLA implementation retains the pretrained expert and action projections and initializes a separate residual network. The draft now describes that mechanism. The attachment's suggested failed alternatives are not presented as experimental evidence because the current manuscript does not provide a controlled comparison supporting them. See `evidence/ACTION_MODULE_AUDIT_20261022.md` and `sections/03_problem_formulation.tex`. |
| A newly initialized head has no “conscious behavior”; dense rewards for complete tasks are highly unlikely to be designed. | The text describes unreliable task-directed behavior and rare terminal successes. Dense full-task rewards are described as difficult and task-specific, consistent with the manuscript's actual LIBERO dense-reward experiment. |
| $p_{\mathrm{total}}$ is at most the product of isolated skill rates. | The product is exact when its factors are the conditional skill probabilities under the same full instruction and actual preceding transitions. Isolated rates from different prompts and prepared resets give neither this identity nor a general upper bound. Self-imitation does not guarantee attaining their product. |
| “Unimodal” dense reward. | No unimodality property is established. The replacement describes a reward which measures progress before success. |
| Curves with $p=[2,4,8,16]$. | These values are group sizes $G$. The probability $p$ ranges from zero to one. The mixed-group formula assumes independent attempts at a fixed scene. |
| GRPO tunes the model to produce informative groups instead of improving task success. | Mixed-group frequency and expected binary reward are different quantities. The draft explains their different dependence on $p$ without attributing mixed-group frequency maximization to GRPO. |
| PLD transfers overlooked visual features without training the base vision encoder. | The retained claim is residual data generation and distillation into the generalist. The cited paper does not establish the specific visual-feature claim as written. |
| All concatenated composition demonstrations are successful episodes generated by the policy itself. | The draft distinguishes relabeled segments, compatible continuous chains, self-generated successes, and scripted/assisted CDPR data. WidowX had 452 source episodes but no continuous successful composition chain. The term SIL refers here to supervised action regression on past policy behavior, not an implementation of the original SIL actor-critic objective. |
| Each phase simply adds +1 in GRPO. | The draft gives the implementation-specific distinction: CDPR compares outcomes separately at milestones and assigns phase-specific advantage; WidowX uses the fraction of milestones reached as its staged return. |
| LIBERO establishes improved individual skills; terminal-only CDPR training does not progress compared with phased reward. | LIBERO measures complete benchmark instructions. Its higher dense-feedback endpoint is reported descriptively. The historical CDPR reward runs have different resets, supervision, and success criteria, so they do not establish a causal reward comparison. |

Routine language corrections include spelling, articles, agreement, punctuation, and tense, for example “exeperiments,” “manipualtors,” “weigths,” “previosly,” the duplicate “provides,” “which progress,” and “a conducted.” Awkward but valid wording was not polished solely for style.
